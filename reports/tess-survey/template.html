Geometry & accuracy

Positions use NASA/JPL Horizons geometric ICRF vectors, with TESS sampled every 3 hours and the Sun daily. Cubic Hermite interpolation uses the supplied velocities. UTC controls are mapped to the ephemeris’s TDB time grid; millisecond time-scale variations are neglected.

Each sector uses MIT’s published J2000 spacecraft RA, declination and roll. The four camera frames and 16 CCD boundaries use the TESS-Point calibrated focal-plane model. CCD gaps are drawn; stellar aberration and instantaneous pointing jitter are omitted.

This is a nominal pointing visualization. Published observing intervals distinguish downlinks and inter-sector gaps where available. The retained field during a gap is a reference only: slews, safe modes, momentum dumps and actual downlink attitude are not reconstructed. Sector 1751 replaces Sector 99 for the published January 15–22, 2026 comet window; those boundaries are date-level estimates.

Earth’s orientation includes IAU 2006 precession and nutation and sidereal spin, approximating UT1 with UTC and omitting polar motion. The Sun uses its IAU pole and a rigid 14.1844°/day reference rotation; its drawn meridians are not measured surface features. Other stars are fixed J2000 catalog directions, not resolved rotating bodies; proper motion and parallax are omitted. The spacecraft shape is schematic; its camera directions use the calibrated frames. Its panels illustrate ideal Sun tracking, not measured panel angles.

Sky-globe and orbit views preserve sight directions. The Sun’s distance and object radii are enlarged or compressed as labeled. In the Sun & Earth view, Earth’s heliocentric position is to scale and TESS’s orbital displacement is enlarged 160×. Camera directions stay inertially fixed.
